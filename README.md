# Savings Tracker (web / phone)

The phone version of the Savings Tracker desktop app: same calculations, same tabs, iOS look.
Data is a JSON file in the user's own Google Drive (or on the device only), synced change by change.

- `index.html`, `css/`, `js/`: the app (plain JavaScript modules, no build step)
- `js/config.js`: your Google sign-in client ID (see SETUP.md)
- `js/core.js`: all calculations, ported line by line from `savings_tracker.py`
- `js/store.js`: Google Drive sync, offline queue, import of the desktop `savings.db`
- `js/car.js` + `js/pages/car.js` (and the `js/pages/car-*.js` next to it: the Log, Charts and Garage tabs, the sheets, tyres, fixed costs, invoice files): the car monitor (the car button in the navigation bar): one or more cars with their details, odometer, fuel, services, inspections, tyres, fixed costs and the purchase papers, saved in the same data file. Its tabs: Car (the car shown, with what's due: tyres, service and inspection), Log, Charts and Garage (all your cars at a glance: specs, fixed costs, purchase). A car can count in the Budget's fixed costs (its fixed costs + fuel a month), so its costs are entered once
- `js/pages/home.js`: Home (the first tab): your bank savings and investments (with how far the buffer is from full), the living money you should have left today (the plan's money a day × the days to payday) with a check-in of what's in your spending account, and the steps that are due: on payday your numbers today (the bank without the salary), the budget, this month's plan and the money to Revolut (the living money to the spending account, and the fixed costs marked Revolut pocket); on the 1st, after the bills, the saving to the bank, investing (only once the buffer is full) and the month's log again; from the 15th, next payday's salary and the costs from new invoices. The living money card also has a chart of what you've spent, from your check-ins, against the budget and the on-track pace (− folds it to a bar of the pay period). Then where this pay period's salary goes, and your one-time expenses. A logged month keeps the day it was logged (`logged`), so Home knows payday numbers from the month logged after the bills
- `js/pages/plan.js`: Plan details (opened from Home, not a tab): pick this month's plan from the budget you saved on the Budget tab (or switch it), the money to move with a tick for each, where the salary goes, what it builds, your action plan, the weeks, the plan's numbers and your past plans. A plan keeps the bank it started from (your payday numbers): from the 1st the bank grows with it, and that isn't a change to the budget
- `js/pages/budget.js` + `js/pages/budget-sheets.js`: the Budget tab, your setup: the salary of this pay period or the next, the fixed costs, the saving goals, the saving plans (each a set amount to spend a week, the rest to your future: Light saving 1 000 kr, Balanced 700 kr, High saving 400 kr, in `PROFILE_DEFS`), a draft to try changes on, and the paydays to come. A fixed cost's amount can change from a payday on, for an invoice that comes in before you pay it with the next salary: the cost's `amount` is the newest one, `history` holds what it was in earlier pay periods and `start_month` the first period it counts in, so the running pay period and its saving plan stay as they are; the ½ button halves the amount typed, for a bill you share. The salary isn't the same every month either: one typed on the Budget tab counts for the pay period shown only (`period_salaries`, by the month of its payday), and the month's log starts from it. A pay period without one takes the salary from its log, or from the latest log until you type it
- `js/pages/onetime.js`: one-time expenses (an air fryer, a flight pass), added on Home and kept in the `one_time` setting with the day they were bought. They're paid from what you already have, so they don't change any budget or plan: they lower what you save that month, and History lists them with that month's log (End of November for a purchase in November)
- `js/pages/draft.js`: Draft budget (from a button at the end of the Budget tab): a copy of the budget to try changes on, kept on the device only; it never changes the real budget
- `js/photos.js`: invoice photos and PDFs, kept on the device until they're in the Google Drive folder
- `vendor/pdfjs/`: PDF.js (Apache 2.0), to show PDF invoices in the app
- `img/car-top.webp`: the car seen from above in Your tyres (its background removed)
- `js/pump.js` + `js/pages/pump-sheet.js`: reading a photo of the pump with Claude (your own API key, kept on the device only): litres, price and amount, checked with litres × price = amount
- `data/fuel-prices.json`: Swedish pump prices for petrol 95 and diesel, every week since 2005 (European Commission, Weekly Oil Bulletin, CC BY 4.0). `tools/fetch_fuel_prices.py` updates it, run every morning by `.github/workflows/fuel-prices.yml`
- `vendor/`: sql.js (MIT licence), used only to read `savings.db` files
- `tests/`: checks for the calculations in `core.js`, with hand-worked answers. Run `npm test` (Node 22+); GitHub runs them on every push

Status: the savings tabs are Home, Budget, History and Insights. Budget, History and Insights work like the desktop app; Home and Plan details are the phone's own (the action plan is on Plan details here). The emergency buffer is set once in Settings: an amount (`buffer_amount`; empty means some months of fixed costs), whether it fills before you invest, and the share that keeps going to savings once it's full.

Setup: see SETUP.md.
